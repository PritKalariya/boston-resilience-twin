# ADR-014: NWS alert history comes from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-26
- Owner: piyush1420
- Phase: data

## Context

The feature table (#16) joins NWS alerts (watches, warnings and advisories) to each neighborhood and day. ADR-003 allows a day-level feature only if its source covers every training year, 2015 to 2023, and asks #12 to find an alert-history source or drop the alert feature. ADR-002 takes live alerts from the NWS API for the event DAG (#7).

Every NWS watch, warning and advisory carries a VTEC code: the alert type (`phenomena`, for example `WS` for winter storm), its level (`significance`: `W` warning, `A` watch, `Y` advisory, `S` statement), the issuing office and an event number. Alerts go to NWS forecast zones and counties (UGC codes). The NWS point lookup puts Boston City Hall in forecast zone MAZ015 (Suffolk) and county MAC025 (Suffolk County), office BOX; all of Boston is in Suffolk County.

The Iowa Environmental Mesonet (IEM) at Iowa State University archives these VTEC events: "All VTEC watch/warn" products from 12 Nov 2005, and tornado, severe thunderstorm and flash flood warnings by county from 1986 (https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/watchwarn.phtml). An alert's start and end can change after it is first sent (extended, upgraded or cancelled); the time of its first bulletin does not. IEM serves the archive in two ways, and only one keeps the first bulletin:

- The per-area service (`https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/json/vtec_events_byugc.py`) answers for one zone or county and gives each alert's start and end in their last state only.
- The bulk download (`https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/request/gis/watchwarn.py`, CSV with `accept=csv`) answers for a forecast office or a state, not a single zone, and also keeps the first bulletin. IEM defines its time columns (https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/info/datasets/vtec.html): `utc_issue` and `utc_expire` are the start and end in the last state; `utc_prodissue` (IEM's `INIT_ISS`) is the "timestamp of the NWS Text Product that started the event"; `utc_init_expire` (`INIT_EXP`) is "the expiration of the event denoted with the first issuance of the event"; `utc_updated` is when the NWS last changed the event. All are UTC. There is no column for the start as first announced.
- For one event at a time, IEM also lists every bulletin with its time and full text, including the VTEC line that gives that bulletin's action and times (`https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/json/vtec_event.py`).

## Decision

- **Source:** the IEM archive of NWS VTEC events, read with its bulk download (`https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/request/gis/watchwarn.py`, `accept=csv`) for office BOX, keeping the rows of Boston's forecast zone MAZ015 and county MAC025. Of IEM's two services it is the one that keeps each alert's first bulletin and first-announced end. The per-area service stays as coverage evidence only: it lists the same Boston alerts, but only in their last state.
- **Coverage:** every year from 2015 to 2026 has Boston alerts, and the archive starts in 2005 (1986 by county for tornado, severe thunderstorm and flash flood warnings). The alert feature meets ADR-003 and is kept. The same archive covers validation (2024), test (2025 to mid-2026) and live, so history and live describe alerts with the same VTEC codes.
- **One rule for history and live (ADR-003 live rule):** an alert counts from its first bulletin, `utc_prodissue` (also the first 12 digits of `product_id`, UTC), until its first-announced end, `utc_init_expire`. It stops counting earlier only when IEM records that it was cancelled or upgraded before the cutoff: `status` is `CAN` or `UPG`, `utc_expire` equals `utc_updated`, and `utc_updated` is before 18:00 `America/New_York` on day D-1. If any of the three fails, the alert keeps its first-bulletin behavior. `status`, `utc_expire` and `utc_updated` describe the last state, so they serve this check and nothing else. Historical features (training, validation and test) and evening features use the same rule.
- **Evening features come from IEM too.** The evening run computes the alert features from a fresh IEM bulk download, with the same code and the same cutoff logic as history, so a rerun of a past evening gives the same features. #7's NWS API feed stays the live source for the event DAG (ADR-002) but does not feed the features: the NWS API keeps no history, so a past evening could not be rebuilt from it.
- **Freshness and clear failure.** The evening run starts with a margin after 18:00 (for example 18:30) and uses only bulletins issued before 18:00. Before using a pull, #16 checks it against the NWS API's active alerts for Boston's zone and county: every alert message sent before 18:00 must already be in the pull. If one is missing, or IEM cannot be reached, the step retries and then fails with a clear error; it never writes zero counts for missing data. Each evening #16 logs how far IEM lagged, so the delay is measured, not assumed.
- **New Year's Eve.** The bulk download picks alerts by start time, so an alert announced on 31 December that starts on 1 January is only in the next year's file. The evening run also pulls the next year's file, at least from late December; fetching the previous year again in January is not needed.
- **Times and the cutoff:** IEM and NWS times are UTC, while our days and the cutoff are Boston local, as everywhere in the pipeline (ADR-020 for 311 times). The forecast for day D uses what is known at 18:00 `America/New_York` on day D-1, computed with a time-zone library (Python `zoneinfo`), never a fixed offset: it is 22:00 UTC under daylight saving time (EDT) and 23:00 UTC otherwise (EST). Clocks change at 02:00, so 18:00 exists exactly once on every day, including the two change days. #16 converts `utc_prodissue`, `utc_init_expire` and `utc_updated` to Boston time before it compares them with the cutoff or assigns them to days. For example, the 2015 blizzard warning, first sent 2015-01-25 20:58 UTC, was sent at 15:58 Boston time, before that evening's 18:00 cutoff, so it is known for the forecast of 26 January.
- **Download and preparation:** #16 downloads the alert history and prepares it for the feature table (see Consequences).
- **Scope:** this ADR picks the source and the fields that are safe to use. Which alert types become features, how they map to storm groups, and how they become daily values belong to #16.

### Limits on preventing future-data leakage

- **No first-announced start.** The bulk download has no start as first announced: `utc_issue` is the start in the last state, in history and in the evening pull alike. It did not move for the 2015 blizzard, but starts can move after the first bulletin; #16 checks how often from the bulletins before it relies on `utc_issue` for a day close to the cutoff.
- **Extensions and other changes before the cutoff are left out, in history and live alike.** Only cancellations and upgrades before the cutoff are used (Decision). An extension, a shortened end or an added area is not: in the 2015 blizzard (Evidence), an update at 17:04 UTC on 27 January moved Boston's end from 28 January 06:00 to 01:00 UTC, before that evening's cutoff, yet the forecast of 28 January still uses 06:00 UTC. These fields never use information from after the cutoff, and since the evening run ignores the same updates, the model learns how far first-announced ends can be trusted. A new alert that replaces another, such as a warning upgraded from a watch, has its own first bulletin and counts on its own.
- **IEM's display advice is the leaky choice.** For display, IEM suggests an event's last end (`EXPIRE`); for features that is exactly the field that leaks.
- **Evening availability is measured, not guaranteed.** IEM is a university service with no uptime guarantee, and one night's measurement (Evidence) shows speed, not reliability. The freshness check and the clear failure (Decision) cover outages; the nightly delay log shows whether the evening run needs a later start.

## Consequences

- #16 may use alerts as a feature. Its leakage test (ADR-003) should count an alert for day D only if its first bulletin is before 18:00 Boston time on day D-1, end it at its first-announced end (`utc_init_expire`), and end it earlier only at a cancellation or upgrade that passes the checks in the Decision. Otherwise the last state is not safe: 62.7% of training-year zone alerts end at a different time than first announced, and 138 zone alerts, mostly watches upgraded or cancelled early, end before they start.
- `utc_init_expire` is written `YYYYMMDDHHMM` (for example `201501280600`), unlike the other time columns, which read `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` (for example `2015-01-27 23:37`), so #16 parses it with its own format. Both are UTC.
- County severe thunderstorm, flash flood and tornado warnings start when first sent, so none is known at an evening cutoff for the next day; their watches can be.
- Alert types change over time, as the 311 categories did. In zone MAZ015, freezing rain advisories appear only in 2015, blizzard watches only up to 2017, flash flood watches up to 2021 (flood watches after), excessive heat up to 2024 (extreme heat from 2025), and wind chill up to 2023 (a cold weather advisory in 2026); county snow squall warnings start in 2019. #16 needs a reviewed, versioned map from VTEC type to storm group, like `docs/data/category-map.csv`.
- Boston is one forecast zone, so zone alerts are the same for all 24 neighborhoods on a day. The alert feature varies by day, not by neighborhood.
- **#16 downloads and prepares the alert history.** It requests the bulk download for office BOX one year at a time, keeps each pull as published under DVC (#18, #19), and prepares one row per first bulletin for zone MAZ015 and county MAC025 with the fields above, keeping `status`, `utc_expire` and `utc_updated` only for the cancellation check. The archive is public and needs no key, so #42's fresh-clone test can rerun the download.
- **#16 still needs the A+ code; evening scheduling is separate.** The committed #16 code implements the first-bulletin rule only: it drops `status`, `utc_expire` and `utc_updated` and pulls years up to the current one. It still needs the cancellation check with its safeguards, the next-year pull, the freshness check and the clear failure, each with tests, including one showing that a cancellation at or after the cutoff changes nothing. Running the alert step each evening after 18:00 belongs to the evening run (#39), not to #16. #7 keeps landing raw NWS alerts for the event DAG (ADR-002) and does not need to keep first versions.
- **Licence and credit.** NWS alerts are U.S. government work: "The information on National Weather Service (NWS) Web pages are in the public domain, unless specifically noted otherwise, and may be used without charge for any lawful purpose so long as you do not: 1) claim it is your own ..., 2) use it in a manner that implies an endorsement or affiliation with NOAA/NWS, or 3) modify its content and then present it as official government material." (https://www.weather.gov/disclaimer). The IEM says: "The materials found on this website are in the public domain and may be used freely by anyone for any lawful purpose. Attributing the Iowa Environmental Mesonet of Iowa State University would be appreciated." The same page gives the data "without any warranty of accuracy" (https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/disclaimer.php). The data card (#43) credits the NWS and the Iowa Environmental Mesonet of Iowa State University. Briefs and dashboards must not present alert-based values as official NWS warnings, in line with the spec: official warnings stay with the City and the NWS.
- Once accepted, this answers ADR-003's "Revisit when #12 finds alert history" and the spec's risk 4 (alert history needs a source). The spec is read-only here; its owner updates risk 4.

## Revisit when

- IEM changes or retires the bulk download or the columns this rule uses (`utc_prodissue`, `utc_init_expire`, `status`, `utc_expire`, `utc_updated`), a cancelled or upgraded Boston row fails the `utc_expire` = `utc_updated` check, or Boston's count drops in a year with known storms.
- #16 finds that starts often move after the first bulletin, or that extensions before the cutoff matter. The team may then move to the latest bulletin before the cutoff (Option B in Alternatives), in history and evening together, once the bulletin history is shown to be complete and correctly timed, the VTEC reconstruction is tested, and the evening inputs match it.
- The nightly freshness log shows IEM late or unavailable at the evening run.
- The NWS changes Boston's zone or county codes, or renames alert types again.
- #16 finds that the alert feature adds nothing beyond the weather forecasts, or cannot pass the leakage test.
